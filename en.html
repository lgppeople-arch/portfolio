Restructuring review of a core business

Building-materials maker · 2022
SITUATION
The group asked whether to keep the core manufacturing business as it was or restructure how it was run.
WHAT I DID
Re-estimated segment revenue and operating profit under both cases, then set out six factors that profitability alone could not settle: operator, business continuity, raw-material supply, sales network, asset value and ancillary assets.
RESULT
The restructuring case showed lower profit and risks that were hard to resolve; it was rejected and the business retained.

TOOLS

Tools you can run in the browser

Calculators built from the way I work, with the assumptions written under each tool. Example figures are illustrative; the holding-company tool can also load LG Corp's disclosed year-end 2025 values. Every page ends with the next step of one of five reading paths (a year of planning and control, one investment, an M&A deal, reading companies through filings, from analysis to reporting), and the full index can be filtered by field. The tools are in Korean.

Investment and M&A

  • Capex appraisal kitNPV, IRR, payback, break-even and two-way sensitivity tables in a spreadsheet-style model. Adds a Monte Carlo NPV distribution and the real-option value of building in two phases.
  • Post-investment reviewPlan versus actual for executed capex, with the variance split into its drivers.
  • M&A process mapThirteen stages and 61 steps from strategy to integration: outputs, owners, gates and the finance checkpoints.
  • M&A valuationDCF and multiples, normalised EBITDA from due diligence, and the bridge from enterprise value to equity price.
  • Holding-company NAVSum-of-the-parts NAV, holding discount, Fair Trade Act holding-company tests and stake-sale scenarios. Opens with LG Corp's disclosed values.
  • FX hedgingForward and option hedges for foreign-currency capex or acquisition payments.

Planning and control

  • KPI tree (ROIC value drivers)ROIC broken down by formula to 13 operating drivers; the plan-versus-actual gap is split across drivers and departments with Shapley values, and drivers are ranked by elasticity.
  • Business-plan risk (Earnings-at-Risk)Monte Carlo of correlated drivers (volume, price, FX, materials) via Cholesky factorisation: probability of hitting the plan, 5th-percentile profit, and an Euler variance decomposition that accounts for correlation.
  • Budget-request prioritisationExpected value from three-point estimates and success probabilities, an exact 0-1 knapsack (dynamic programming) under the approval limit, risk-adjusted certainty equivalents, and the marginal value of one more unit of budget.
  • Monthly P&L reportBuilds the report from SAP-style data, with variance analysis against plan, a seasonally adjusted trend line with ±1σ bands and outlier flags, and a t-test for systematic plan bias.
  • Cost behaviour and defect control chartsFixed and variable cost by regression with confidence intervals, a Chow test for a structural break after a line expansion, p-charts with Nelson rules and EWMA, and a two-proportion test of a process fix.
  • MBO and KPI evaluationTarget setting, scoring and incentive allocation, plus distribution analysis of results: dispersion, central tendency, a chi-square test against the grade guide, rater bias by ANOVA, z-score calibration and a Gini check on incentives.
  • Delegation-of-authority checkerChecks approval limits against board thresholds and Commercial Act requirements.
  • 13-week cash flow and covenantsBusiness-day rules, stress cases and revolving-credit draws, with a Monte Carlo run for the probability of needing the facility, exhausting it, or running out of cash.
  • Debt capacity and default riskCash-flow based probability of a debt-service shortfall, Merton distance to default, debt capacity under four policy limits, the trade-off between tax shields and distress costs, and Altman Z′.

Every calculation is covered by identity tests that run in the browser: check page.
